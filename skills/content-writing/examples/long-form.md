# Long-form example

This is an original composite showing the shape of an evidence-led newsletter or article.

## Newsletter/article — opening and outline

**Route:** newsletter/article × tech productivity × argument

### Title

Your AI workflow does not need more context. It needs better boundaries.

### Deck

Why adding documents can make an assistant less reliable—and how to separate durable rules, current
state, and task-specific evidence.

### Opening

The first version of an AI workspace usually feels magical. Add the strategy deck, customer notes,
past projects, brand guidance, and operating rules. Suddenly the assistant can answer questions that
once required searching five systems.

Then the answers begin to drift.

One document says the launch is in September; another records the revised October date. A temporary
preference becomes a permanent rule. The assistant spends more time reconciling context than solving
the task.

The natural response is to organize harder or add an even larger knowledge base. But the deeper
problem is not missing context. It is that information with different jobs and lifetimes has been
placed in the same layer.

Reliable AI workspaces separate three things: durable instructions, current working state, and
evidence retrieved for the task at hand.

### Body outline

1. **Why more context eventually creates conflicts** — explain recency, authority, and retrieval.
2. **Layer one: durable rules** — what belongs; what becomes harmful when frozen as a rule.
3. **Layer two: working state** — decisions, owners, dates, blockers, and expiry.
4. **Layer three: task evidence** — retrieve a small source packet instead of loading everything.
5. **The boundary test** — ask how often information changes and which tasks require it.
6. **Limitations** — when a larger fixed context is still the simpler choice.
7. **Conclusion** — better boundaries make less context more useful.

**Why it works:** the opening uses a recognizable progression, names the actual mechanism, and earns
the thesis before introducing the framework.
