# Social-post examples

These are original composites. They demonstrate reusable structural choices; they are not copies or
imitations.

Provenance note: the structural annotations were informed by saved public-post collections across
technical, product, and creator-business writing. No source wording is reproduced.

## X short post — informative tech

**Route:** X short post × informative tech × contrarian argument

Most AI workflow failures are blamed on the model.

But the model is often the least mysterious part.

The workflow has no clear source of truth, nobody owns the review, and exceptions have nowhere to
go. A smarter model only reaches the same ambiguity faster.

Before changing models, define the input, owner, approval point, and failure path.

**Why it works:** the opening names a familiar diagnosis, the middle supplies a mechanism, and the
ending converts the argument into a decision rule.

## X thread — tech productivity

**Route:** X thread × tech productivity × listicle

**1/6**

An AI assistant becomes genuinely useful when it remembers how work should move—not merely what you
asked in the last message.

Five pieces turn isolated prompts into a repeatable workflow:

**2/6 — A stable brief**

Keep the objective, audience, constraints, and definition of done in one canonical place. If the
brief changes in every chat, consistency is impossible.

**3/6 — Approved sources**

Name where facts may come from. Separate source material from the agent's inference so reviewers can
challenge either one.

**4/6 — An output contract**

Define the artifact: its sections, level of detail, and decision it should enable. “Analyze this” is
not an output contract.

**5/6 — Review gates**

Mark the moments that require judgment: accepting a claim, changing a customer record, publishing,
or spending money.

**6/6 — A learning loop**

Record corrections that should apply next time. Do not encode every preference after one example;
promote only patterns that repeat.

The goal is not maximum autonomy. It is dependable progress with clear ownership.

**Why it works:** the opening promises a defined set, every post adds a distinct mechanism, and the
ending synthesizes rather than repeating the list.

## LinkedIn — operating framework

**Route:** LinkedIn post × AI automation × explainer

Buying a better AI model rarely fixes a workflow nobody owns.

When an automation fails, inspect five things before touching the prompt:

1. **Trigger:** What starts the workflow?
2. **Input:** Which source is authoritative?
3. **Decision:** What may the model infer, and what must be deterministic?
4. **Review:** Who approves costly or irreversible actions?
5. **Failure path:** Where do exceptions go, and who resolves them?

A model can improve classification, extraction, or drafting. It cannot decide your operating model
for you.

Treat AI quality and workflow quality as separate systems. Measure both.

**Why it works:** it frames a professional misconception, provides a memorable framework, and lands
on an operational distinction instead of engagement bait.
