# Corpus, provenance, and voice

Use saved posts, bookmarks, transcripts, articles, and voice samples as evidence—not as a persona to
clone.

## Build a source packet

Choose three to seven items that match the requested format, subject, or rhetorical pattern. Prefer:

1. the user's own approved writing and brand guidance;
2. the user's curated saves and notes;
3. complete primary sources and transcripts;
4. current, authoritative sources for factual product claims;
5. external examples only for a gap the existing packet does not cover.

Record enough provenance to find the source again: creator/author, title or short identifier, URL or
local source ID, date, format, and why it was selected.

## Extract decisions, not mannerisms

For each item, note reusable editorial choices:

- hook type and what makes it credible;
- thesis placement;
- information order;
- evidence type;
- sentence and paragraph rhythm;
- use of examples, lists, questions, and transitions;
- how the ending resolves the opening.

Generalize a pattern only when it appears across several examples or when the user explicitly
selects it. One memorable post is inspiration, not a rule.

## Handle creator-style requests

Do not reproduce a living creator's distinctive voice, catchphrases, or recognizable phrasing.
Translate the request into high-level traits such as:

- concise or expansive;
- analytical or conversational;
- evidence-led or narrative-led;
- fast-paced or reflective;
- tutorial, review, listicle, or argument;
- frequent or restrained use of examples, humor, and calls to action.

Apply those traits to the user's topic and voice. Keep creator names in a private provenance ledger,
not in route names, reusable style labels, or shipped examples.

## Protect private material

- Keep raw exports, full transcripts, private bookmarks, and analysis reports outside a public skill.
- Do not reproduce long passages from saved sources.
- Use short attributed excerpts only when necessary to analyze a specific choice.
- Ship original composite examples and generalized patterns.
- Preserve source links privately so claims and interpretations can be audited.
