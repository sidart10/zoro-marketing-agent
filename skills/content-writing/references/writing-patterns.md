# Writing patterns and subject lenses

Select a subject lens and one primary rhetorical pattern. These are reasoning structures, not
fill-in-the-blank templates.

## Subject lenses

### Tech productivity

- Begin with the work or decision, not the tool.
- Show the before/after workflow and where human judgment remains.
- Prefer time saved, rework avoided, context preserved, or decisions improved over vague “10x” claims.
- Explain setup and maintenance costs alongside the payoff.

### Informative tech

- Define unfamiliar terms at first use.
- Move from mental model to concrete example to boundary.
- Separate current capability from demo potential and speculation.
- Use official documentation or primary evidence for product claims when available.

### Tech product review or comparison

- State the audience and decision being made.
- Choose criteria before reaching the verdict.
- Compare evidence under the same conditions.
- Name tradeoffs, failure modes, price assumptions, and who should choose differently.

### AI automation

- Identify trigger, inputs, transformation, output, owner, and failure path.
- Separate deterministic steps from model judgment.
- Mark approvals before payments, publishing, deletion, customer commitments, or irreversible actions.
- Explain monitoring and maintenance; a generated workflow is not automatically an operated system.

### Creator or business education

- Connect tactic to the underlying economic or audience mechanism.
- Use a real example or transparent hypothetical.
- Distinguish repeatable process from survivorship-biased outcome.
- End with a decision rule the audience can apply.

## Rhetorical patterns

### Explainer

Question or confusion → mental model → concrete example → boundary → takeaway.

Best when the audience needs understanding before action.

### Listicle

Specific promise → inclusion criteria → ordered items → synthesis.

Each item should add a different mechanism or decision, not restate the same advice. Order by
importance, sequence, or difficulty and tell the reader which logic you chose.

### Tutorial

Outcome → prerequisites → steps → checkpoints → common failure → next action.

Show what success looks like after important steps. Explain why a step exists when skipping it would
cause a predictable failure.

### Comparison or review

Decision → criteria → evidence by criterion → tradeoffs → verdict by audience.

Avoid a universal winner when the evidence supports different choices for different users.

### Contrarian argument

Common belief → precise disagreement → mechanism/evidence → boundary → implication.

Disagree with an idea, not a caricature. A useful contrarian piece narrows the claim and explains
what most people miss; it does not simply reverse conventional advice.

### Case study or build log

Initial state → constraint → action → observed result → interpretation → reusable lesson.

Separate measured results from the author's explanation of why they happened. Include the parts that
did not work when they materially affect the lesson.

### Narrative lesson

Scene → tension → choice or turning point → consequence → meaning → application.

Use only real experiences supplied by the user or clearly labeled composites/hypotheticals. Never
invent a first-person anecdote to make the lesson feel authentic.
